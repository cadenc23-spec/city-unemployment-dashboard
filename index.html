U.S. city labor market brief · 2021–2025

Unemployment across 50 large U.S. cities

This report compares annual unemployment rates across 50 cities and summarizes how the cross-city average changed from 2021 through 2025. The figures describe labor-market conditions; they do not, by themselves, determine whether a city is suitable for business expansion.

2025 coverage: The 2025 figures are 11-month averages. October data were not collected during the federal government shutdown, so 2025 is not a full calendar-year average.

How to read these results

Each record is one city in one year. Annual means give each city equal weight because each city contributes one observation per year. City averages are arithmetic means of that city's five annual rates. Rates are shown as percentages; differences between rates are described in percentage points where appropriate.

All 250 supplied rates are present and numeric. No rows were excluded for missing values. The dataset contains 50 unique cities and one observation for every city in each year from 2021 to 2025. Its rounded input rates support descriptive comparisons, not causal conclusions.

Unemployment is one input to expansion analysis. An actual location decision also depends on factors such as workforce skills, wages, costs, customer demand, infrastructure, and the business's operating model.
